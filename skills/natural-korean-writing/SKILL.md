# Natural Korean Writing

Write Korean that fits its reader without changing the source's meaning, ownership, certainty, or intended act. This skill covers drafting, revision, and review; it is not a full-document translation or fact-checking service.

## Resolve scope before style

Use the latest explicit instruction for the same target and scope. Distinguish the chat reply, the reader-facing artifact, and protected spans; a change to one does not automatically apply to the others. A general style request does not unlock approved wording. A specific later authorization changes only the identified span.

Preserve user-confirmed wording exactly, including spaces and punctuation. Protect code, identifiers, filenames, paths, URLs, commands, options, branches, versions, regular expressions, API values, and official names or source quotations that must be reproduced exactly. Quotation marks delimiting an editable draft do not lock that draft. An ordinary explanatory mention of a product is not automatically an exact official-name field; apply the source and the user's naming instruction to its actual role.

Keep the inspection scope separate from the edit scope. Reading the whole document does not authorize changing the whole document. In a local edit, keep all text outside the authorized spans unchanged, even if it was not separately marked approved. If two explicit requirements cannot both be met, such as a whole-output string ban overlapping a required literal, identify that narrow conflict rather than silently changing or omitting the literal.

## Preserve the claim while improving the sentence

1. Preserve facts, measurements and their conditions, responsibility, terminology, motives, qualifications, uncertainty, and commitments. Use supplied context and direct corrections to resolve a misleading draft; do not preserve its mistake merely because it appeared first.
2. Distinguish learning, personal use, implementation, testing, release, and observed effects. A request, plan, test source, or screen observation is not evidence of execution, a passing run, persistence, or a wider result.
3. Identify the supported actor, action, object, and completed, ongoing, or planned state. Use a concrete verb when the source supports it; do not invent a method or result to replace vague wording such as `처리했습니다` or `검증했습니다`. Keep those words when they accurately express the action.
4. Check that each predicate fits its subject, object, particles, and complements. Omit an established subject naturally; identify a changed actor or referent before responsibility becomes ambiguous.
5. Keep the relationship and speech act intact. Check endings, pronouns, address forms, and request strength together. Polite chat stays polite; casual chat does not become formal merely because the skill also covers external documents.
6. Preserve the source-supported reason linking facts to a choice or claim. Chronology is not causation, a credential is not a study process, and a general ability claim is not a concrete action. Do not invent a motive, lesson, benefit, or commitment to make a paragraph persuasive.
7. Shorten redundancy, not a central strength, a required answer, a material limitation, or a needed transition. Modesty must not erase supported competence, and smooth wording must not turn intention into achievement.
8. Treat noun stacks, passive voice, demonstratives, repeated endings, and abstract nouns as review signals, not universal bans. Keep established professional terms when an invented translation would be less precise.
9. Review Korean originals in Korean. For a translation, check against the actual source separately and reread the Korean alone. Never invent an English original or use a plausible back-translation as proof of naturalness.

## Deliver the requested result

Separate usable copy from commentary about it. A request for words to say, send, or post normally needs one settled version per situation, not drafting notes, self-evaluation, unsolicited alternatives, or follow-up offers. A request for review or explanation needs the requested reasoning. Essential unresolved meaning still warrants a focused clarification rather than fabricated final copy.

After successive edits, return the complete affected field when requested, with all accepted changes assembled. Preserve the destination's format. Labels and titles may be short phrases; explanatory prose must make the action and target clear. Do not force sentence endings or question-shaped labels into every table cell.

## Final pass

Check the exact outgoing version, including headings, notes, and closing remarks:

1. Compare it with the source, latest corrections, approved text, and authorized edit spans. Check omissions as well as substitutions.
2. Read in the intended reader's actual order using the context companion. The necessary subject, reason, condition, and conclusion must be in the passage, not only in private notes.
3. Check facts and logic, then make a separate Korean-language pass for predicate compatibility, register, referents, and transitions. Recheck meaning after changing the language.
4. Apply punctuation, protected-text and naming rules, scoped restrictions, and the requested format to the assembled output. Use the editing contract for detailed checks.
5. Remove unrequested process commentary. If a material issue remains, identify it instead of calling the result finished. Claim reading, review, execution, saving, or submission only when the corresponding action was actually verified.

### Independent review when requested or established

An applicable established requirement for independent review remains active through revisions. Follow the [editing and review contract](references/editing-and-review.md#independent-review-and-final-version) for the actual materials, reviewer handoff, findings, and final-version comparison. Self-review, a pending request, or a review of an earlier draft does not satisfy it.
