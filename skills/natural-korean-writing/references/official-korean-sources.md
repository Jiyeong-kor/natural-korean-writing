# Official Korean sources

Choose evidence for the specific uncertainty. Ordinary revisions do not require a lookup for every sentence. When verification is needed, open the relevant official page and inspect the entry or rule itself; a search snippet is not enough. Summarize the principle in your own words and link to it without copying long passages.

## Word meaning and sentence patterns

Use the [표준국어대사전](https://stdict.korean.go.kr/main/main.do) for definitions, part of speech, inflection, sentence patterns, grammar notes, and examples. Its [도움말](https://stdict.korean.go.kr/help/helpList.do) explains the available search fields.

Check which complements a predicate requires and which sense matches the intended action. A dictionary entry does not guarantee that a word sounds natural in every genre. Conversely, an established technical term is not wrong merely because the dictionary does not list it. Preserve precise terminology that the reader knows; explain it when the reader needs help.

## Spelling and punctuation

Use [한국어 어문 규범](https://korean.go.kr/kornorms/main/main.do) for spelling, spacing, standard-language forms, loanword spelling, and Romanization. For punctuation, consult the [한글 맞춤법 부록: 문장 부호](https://korean.go.kr/kornorms/regltn/regltnView.do?regltn_code=0001&regltn_no=714).

Distinguish the rule, an allowed alternative, and commentary about specialist notation. Apply [korean-punctuation-rules.md](korean-punctuation-rules.md) to the editable Korean while preserving exact source text. Norms settle form; they do not choose the reader, relationship, or appropriate amount of information.

## Clear public writing

The National Institute of Korean Language's [한눈에 알아보는 공공언어 바로 쓰기(개정판)](https://www.korean.go.kr/front/etcData/etcDataView.do?etc_seq=699&mn_id=208&pageIndex=2) links to a guide covering clear word choice, sentence-component agreement, modifiers, omissions, translation-like expressions, and logical paragraph connections. Use the relevant explanation to diagnose a concrete clarity problem.

Treat public-language guidance as evidence for clarity, not a requirement to turn conversations, UI labels, or technical documents into administrative prose. Read the applicable section before relying on its advice. Link to the guide instead of reproducing its pages, examples, or layout; respect the publication's reuse conditions.

## Usage evidence from corpora

[모두의 말뭉치 소개](https://kli.korean.go.kr/m/introduce/corpusIntroduce.do) describes written and spoken data. Its [자주 찾는 질문](https://kli.korean.go.kr/m/boards/faqList.do) explains access and publication conditions. Downloads require registration, application approval, and agreement to the terms. Check those terms before using data; the FAQ requires attribution and prior approval for external publication of corpus-based results.

Use corpus evidence only when the relevant data is available under its terms and has actually been inspected. Identify the dataset, genre, period, query, and comparison basis before claiming a frequency or usage trend. Do not infer a trend from the portal description, an uninspected download, or a few search examples. If the data is unavailable, make no corpus-backed claim.

## Keep research within the writing task

Use public pages and documents when needed. Do not add an API client, request credentials, enroll for restricted data, or build live lookup infrastructure merely to revise Korean. If a source cannot be inspected, state the narrow uncertainty when it affects the answer; do not invent evidence or change verified technical meaning.
