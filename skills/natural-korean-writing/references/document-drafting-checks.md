## Shared document drafting checks

When a document needs a title, keep it brief and specific to its content. In reports and decision-oriented documents, normally lead with the conclusion or requested action, then give the supporting reasons and necessary detail. Honor the user's required order and retain context needed to interpret the conclusion; do not mechanically move a conclusion ahead of its essential qualification or impose this structure on every conversation or narrative.

Prefer Hangul in ordinary Korean prose when Hanja characters add no needed distinction. Preserve characters needed for an exact official name, quotation, protected literal, or meaningful disambiguation. This concerns the use of Hanja characters, not a ban on Korean words of Chinese origin written in Hangul.

Prefer a direct statement of the supported action over an unnecessary negative or double-negative construction when the meaning and force remain the same. A positive sentence form is not a reason to hide a negative fact, prohibition, failed action, limitation, or uncertainty. Do not delete negation mechanically, weaken an obligation, invent an available action, or turn a plan into a completed result.

Include a preparation date when the requested document format, record keeping, or interpretation requires it, using an appropriate date field or metadata. Use the confirmed date for the event that field names: preparation, revision, issue, submission, and an activity date are not interchangeable. Do not overwrite a historical preparation date with the current date merely while polishing the text. If a necessary date is unknown, ask or identify that gap instead of inventing it. Do not add a date to every ordinary chat reply.

### Plan and verify required evidence

Before drafting, identify what the reader needs to understand or assess this document type. Distinguish input and source materials, methods and conditions, results and limits, comparison figures or tables, and source and reproduction records. Map the necessary components to their intended locations in the body or permitted supporting materials, then compare that map with what is actually present. This is a working check, not a mandatory visible checklist or a requirement to include every component in every document.

For an analysis of visual input, such as an OCR experiment report, place the actual input images and supported representative before-and-after or success-and-failure examples in the body, paired with the corresponding outputs and explanations. Retain the context needed to interpret each example. A downloadable archive can hold originals and reproduction materials, but does not replace the visual evidence the reader needs beside the analysis. Do not invent missing images, processing stages, or results, or present an illustration as an actual experimental input. Preserve who performed the work; an assistant or tool run must not become the user's own experiment or achievement.

Apply this check within the document's format and disclosure rules. A text-only form does not acquire image fields, and blind review or restricted publication does not authorize exposing identifying evidence. Use a permitted separate evidence location when appropriate; if necessary evidence cannot be included or accessed there, state the limitation without claiming the document is complete. Follow the existing [authorized form-work guidance](application-answer-workflow.md#coordinate-writing-and-authorized-form-work) for required and optional application attachments rather than imposing a second attachment checklist.

After an authorized save, reopen the actual destination or exported document in the intended reader view where access permits. Compare the required components with their saved locations. Check that images actually load, that each image, caption, output, and body explanation refer to the same example, and that the displayed size and resolution let the reader interpret the result. An upload response, image URL, editor preview, or ZIP alone does not establish this. Repair missing, mismatched, or unreadable evidence within scope and repeat the affected checks. If the saved view is unavailable, distinguish the prepared content from the unverified saved result and identify the pending checks.
