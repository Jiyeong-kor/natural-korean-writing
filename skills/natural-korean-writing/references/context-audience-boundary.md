# Context and Audience Boundary

Read this mandatory bundled companion before any Korean drafting, revision, or review, including a short reply. Reuse established context and reconsider what changed. Keep these checks private unless an explanation is requested; they are not a questionnaire or a template to paste into the result. Apply the entrypoint's instruction scope and protected-text rules throughout.

## Establish the speaking situation

Recover only the distinctions the request needs:

1. **Speaker:** Whose words are these? Separate the requester, assistant, intended sender, person described, and group. First-person claims and promises belong to the represented speaker, not whoever supplied the facts.
2. **Recipient and relationship:** Use the established familiarity, role, formality, and destination. Do not infer a person's name, age, gender, rank, or intimacy. Align endings, pronouns, address forms, and request strength; a polite ending alone does not establish a polite sentence.
3. **Time and stage:** Separate event time, sending time, and reading time; planned, ongoing, and completed actions; initial contact, a reply, and an unanswered follow-up. Resolve relative dates against the supplied setting, not the machine's date. Keep a supplied year when the result and explicitly shared context would not identify it. Do not add urgency, elapsed time, immediacy, or a deadline. Choose tense per claim, not for the whole paragraph.
4. **Place, channel, and access:** Determine which space, screen, object, prior message, or visible field the recipient actually shares. A gesture or `여기` may work in person but not on the phone. Use known identifiers when necessary; do not invent them. Public visibility does not make everyone strangers, and familiarity does not authorize public disclosure.
5. **Intended act:** Preserve whether the text asks, suggests, requests, refuses, apologizes, reports, or commits. Availability is not an arrangement; intention is not completion. Politeness must not weaken a required action or add a promise. Drafting a request does not authorize performing it.
6. **Reader knowledge:** Use context actually available to the reader. Omit a subject or explanation only when its referent stays clear, and identify actor changes. A writer's prior chats or files do not by themselves supply that context.

Keep chat style separate from artifact style. Different audiences may need different wording and explanation, but not different facts, ownership, or unauthorized commitments.

### Keep the person's perspective in personal explanations

For a motive, choice, or judgment, distinguish external circumstances, the person's supported observation and interpretation or forecast, and the stated choice. Make their supported relationship readable. If the request calls for historical, social, or technological background, establish the external condition or change from source material covering that period. A personal life event, its date, or the person's prediction does not by itself establish that external background. Do not project later conditions into an earlier decision or turn a forecast into an observed change. Public facts or product features plus `저는` do not establish a personal reason. Knowing, reading, witnessing, using, and interpreting are different actions; the assistant's research proves none of them about the represented person. Do not infer personal causation from external evidence alone or require historical background for every personal explanation.

If the personal basis is missing, retrieve an authorized source or identify that specific gap. Do not invent feelings or a causal story. Retain institution or object subjects for genuine public facts and functions, and omit an already clear personal subject naturally.

## Introduce unfamiliar subjects before relying on them

Give the minimum verified description before or alongside an unfamiliar target's first needed use. A project's name alone may not identify its purpose. Preserve the relevant function and personal role; do not imply ownership of the whole team's work or add irrelevant team size, release state, or feature lists. Never guess a purpose from a name.

Determine the actual reading unit before repeating background. An adjacent visible field may already identify the project, even if the current answer is a separate field. A later answer, unopened attachment, or independently displayed excerpt may not. A field boundary alone proves neither shared context nor independence. If the layout is unknown, use a brief supported description when identification matters; ask only when the uncertainty materially changes the answer.

Fictional example: an isolated answer about `모아결제` may need `간편결제 앱` at first mention. If the adjacent visible field already gives that description, repeating it is unnecessary. If the team developed payment functionality and the writer handled only its error screen, preserve both roles rather than crediting the writer with the whole app.

### Review in the reader's actual order

Start with what this reader already knows, then read from the beginning using only information available at each point. Find the first sentence that relies on an unexplained target, actor, reason, or condition. Repair that dependency in the actual passage, within the allowed edit scope, and reread from the beginning after moving or shortening text.

Do not impose background-first prose universally. A clear answer-first lead, intended suspense, and professional shared knowledge can be appropriate. Do not repeat familiar terms or explain every noun. If a necessary repair falls outside a local edit, report that boundary rather than rewriting other passages without permission.

## Decide what belongs in the result

Check relevance separately from truth. Keep information that the reader needs and is authorized to receive.

1. **Need:** Would removing this clause lose a required answer, reason, action, outcome, strength, risk, or material qualification? Keep that substance. Omit irrelevant strategy, discarded drafts, inspection history, and repeated background; do not revive a deliberately removed claim with synonyms.
2. **Permission:** Private source material is not permission to disclose personal details, internal identifiers, operational figures, or partial results to another audience. Resolve unclear necessary disclosure before publishing it.
3. **Precision:** Preserve dates, years, units, time standards, identifiers, calculation bases, and comparison conditions when they affect interpretation, action, validity, deadlines, reproducibility, or the requested audit. Omit a condition only when the reader's explicitly shared context already supplies it, not merely because the writer knows it.
4. **Truthfulness:** Omission must not turn partial work into completion, a proposal into implementation, or a limited observation into a general result. Narrow an unsupported claim or state the limitation separately; do not hide it under a rule against internal detail.

Internal analysis, change history, rationale, or evidence can belong in a requested review or report. It does not automatically belong in final copy. Apply this distinction to the whole response, including anything appended after the usable text.

A form-entry limitation is usually process context, not the activity itself. State the actual ongoing or completed activity; retain a separate necessary qualification if a required date would otherwise mislead. A test's expected value is not a passing result. Keep necessary evidence without narrating every source lookup.

## Explain the problem before proposing a priority

When the question requests a problem or improvement priority, connect the user's task, a supported obstacle, its consequence, and the reason for that priority in the actual passage. Separate current behavior, observed difficulty, possible effects, and proposed remedies. An existing feature or deliberate limit is not automatically a defect. Do not infer absence, poor comprehension, measured harm, or the largest problem from a plausible story. Use the proposal profile for detailed proposal checks, and add a remedy or assessment plan only when requested.

## Handle missing context proportionately

Use what is already supplied. Keep information unknown to the assistant distinct from the user's stated indecision, willingness to negotiate, refusal to disclose, choice to omit, or not-applicable status. Not asking, receiving no answer, or failing to access a source establishes none of those positions or the absence of a preference. Respect each explicit answer within its scope; stated indecision is not negotiation or omission consent. If a required presentation choice remains unresolved, clarify only that choice. Reuse authorized original material when its target, time, and scope apply; do not automatically transplant a value from another application or document. Ask only when missing information changes meaning, responsibility, disclosure, or commitment. Otherwise use wording that avoids the unsupported assumption and still answers the request. Even an answer-only request does not justify inventing essential meaning; state the smallest necessary clarification.

## Carry the question through revisions

Keep the exact question and latest applicable corrections active. Each paragraph must serve the current request. Facts alone do not supply a missing reason or transition, and an internal explanation cannot repair the submitted text. A new example or future plan needs a source-supported connection, not just a connective word.

## Check the actual wording

Read each audience's version as its intended recipient, at the supplied time and place. Check ownership, requested response, disclosed information, date identification, and unfamiliar targets at their first needed use. Repair only within the authorized scope, remove needless repetition, and deliver the requested text rather than the checks above.
