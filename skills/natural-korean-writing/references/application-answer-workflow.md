# Application Answer Workflow

Read this entire reference when drafting, revising, or reviewing application answers. Apply it with the entrypoint's required context and punctuation guidance and the application prose profile. Keep working records private unless the user requests them. These are decisions tied to the actual application, not headings to paste into every answer. All examples in this reference are fictional.

## Select experiences against the complete prompt

Recover each exact question and all accompanying guidance, required subquestions, length limits, and counting rules from the supplied application. Preserve their wording in the working record; a shortened title such as `지원동기` is not a substitute for the full prompt. Map each requested element to the evidence and answer passage that will address it before drafting. Do not silently turn employer guidance into an optional suggestion or invent unpublished selection criteria.

When the user points to an earlier explanation or says the draft misses their meaning, read the relevant accessible original account before rewriting the story. Separate the user's words from an assistant's prior interpretation, and apply later direct corrections first. Search results or summaries are leads, not substitutes for the relevant original passage. If that passage is inaccessible, identify the narrow meaning still unresolved rather than inventing continuity or asking the user to repeat information already accessible. This does not authorize broader disclosure or make private context visible to the final reader.

Before selecting from a portfolio or repository collection, establish the requested source scope and the current official requirements and duties the examples must support. When the user asks to consider all their GitHub repositories, inventory the authorized collection, screen each available repository for relevant personal evidence, and inspect the underlying material for plausible candidates. Keep a private record of sources inspected, excluded with a supported reason, inaccessible, and not yet inspected. Reuse verified records where still applicable, but a keyword search or a familiar project alone does not establish whole-collection coverage. Do not claim exhaustive selection while requested sources remain unexamined; identify that limit while continuing supported work. This does not authorize unrelated access or require every repository to appear in the application. Compare plausible experiences against the question map before choosing one. When several supported candidates exist, compare their coverage, the applicant's actual contribution, available evidence, and overlap with the other answers. A technically impressive experience can be a poor fit for the question. If only one supported candidate exists, assess its gaps without inventing a second experience. If a required element remains unsupported, retrieve available material or identify the missing fact; do not disguise the gap with a fluent sentence. Keep this comparison out of the submitted answer unless requested.

For a company-specific motive or plan, identify the official company material and current role posting that support the claim, along with their date and role scope. Use available authorized sources; if current evidence cannot be checked, qualify the claim or ask for the missing source. An old experienced-hire posting, a third-party summary, or a plausible technical inference does not establish the current entry-level role's stack or duties. Link the supported company characteristic to the applicant's own confirmed interest, experience, or decision: company praise and a separate personal anecdote do not by themselves explain why this person chose this company. Keep source links in the working evidence or requested review; include them in the submitted field only when that format calls for them.

Check personal records against the applicant's own evidence. For education, use the specific enrollment or completion record for that person and cohort. A current course page does not establish a past cohort's name, dates, hours, completion, or attendance. Never manufacture a cohort number or infer it from an adjacent program or calendar year.

Distinguish material supporting an account of learning or work from proof accepted by the receiving institution. A file's existence does not establish that it meets the current application instructions for issuer, document type, or required contents. Check those requirements before describing a record as accepted proof; keep an unconfirmed acceptance status separate from the facts the material actually supports.

For a motivation answer, start from the applicant's confirmed choices, experiences, and reasons, then connect them to the supported work or goal. A list of company duties followed by `제게 맞습니다` is not that connection.

Before drafting a motivation passage that moves from education or an award to a different technical field and then to a project, identify the actual relationship for **each transition**. Confirm why the applicant began the additional study, what specific knowledge or method they later used, and which problem in the project required it. Chronology alone does not establish that language study caused AI interest, that an award proves a technical skill, or that a course led to a project. If the interests developed independently, describe that honestly and show the first confirmed activity where they came together; remove a background detail if it never affects the applicant's choice. A connective such as `이후`, `또한`, `바탕으로`, or `연결됩니다` cannot supply an absent reason.

When describing technical evidence in a motivation answer, link consecutive sentences as **task → obstacle and why it matters → applicant's judgment and action → known outcome or present question**. For example, a bare observation that OCR returned extraneous screen text does not explain why the applicant filtered it: name the downstream task and the risk of sending irrelevant text before explaining the chosen filter. Do not manufacture a failed model prediction, measured accuracy gain, or historical interest to complete that chain. During the final pass, ask of each sentence: `What makes this fact relevant to the next sentence and to this applicant's reason for applying?` Repair the missing relationship in the prose itself; do not merely add transition words or explain the reason outside the draft.


### Remove job-posting recaps from motivation endings

Before delivering a motivation answer, inspect its closing sentences for a standalone description of what the employer or its project does, especially a template such as `[Company]'s project collects A and compares B.` A correct summary of the posting is not the applicant's reason for choosing it. If removing that sentence leaves the applicant's own experience, question, choice, and reason unchanged, delete it. If the reader needs some duty context, integrate only the necessary part into the applicant's confirmed interest or concrete reason; do not add a separate recitation followed by a generic `그래서 지원했습니다`. The final passage should answer why this person wants to examine this particular problem, not merely prove they read the job description.

Fictional example, assuming the applicant has explicitly confirmed that interest:

- Weak: `회사의 연구 과제는 고객 문의를 유형별로 나누고 분류 결과를 비교합니다. 이러한 업무에 관심이 있어 지원했습니다.`
- Better: `비슷한 표현이 문의 상황에 따라 다른 유형으로 분류되는 이유가 궁금합니다. 판단이 엇갈리는 사례를 모아 어떤 문맥이 분류를 바꾸는지 확인해 보고 싶습니다.`

This check applies even when the company-summary sentence is fluent and factually accurate. Do not ban the grammatical ending `~합니다` or all descriptions of the employer; retain them when the reader genuinely needs the fact. Do not invent longstanding interest, completed experiments, or personal motives to replace a weak recap.


### Replace label-style résumé bridges with connected actions

Before delivering an application answer, review transitions for repeated category-label declarations such as `언어와 AI를 함께 다룬 작업은 ○○ 프로젝트였습니다`, `이 경험은 ○○ 역량입니다`, or `○○는 ○○였습니다`. Such sentences can sound like a résumé heading followed by a list of facts. They are especially weak when the user expects an account of why one event led to the next. Do not try to repair them by adding `이후`, `한편`, or a generic `이 경험을 바탕으로`; replace the label with the actual action that brought the topics together, the obstacle and consequence, and the applicant's judgment. The reader should be able to answer why this detail follows the previous one without relying on an explanation outside the draft.

Fictional example:

- Weak: `언어와 AI를 함께 다룬 경험은 뉴스 분류 프로젝트였습니다. OCR의 문제는 광고 문구가 함께 나온다는 점이었습니다.`
- Better: `Python을 공부한 뒤 뉴스 분류 프로젝트에서 기사 이미지를 분류 모델의 입력으로 가공했습니다. OCR 결과에 광고 문구까지 섞여 본문과 무관한 글자가 모델에 들어갈 수 있었으므로, 본문 영역을 골라낼 기준을 정했습니다.`

This is a check against mechanical exposition, not a ban on `~은`, `~는`, or `~입니다` as Korean grammar. Keep an identification sentence only when it is genuinely needed for clarity and immediately supports the explanation. Do not infer unsupported causality between unrelated education, independent interests, awards, and technical projects; show the confirmed point where they were used together.


### Apply the author's accepted line edits as style evidence

When the author edits a draft and instructs the assistant to keep that version, compare the previous draft with the edited text and retain the user's exact changes. Infer a reusable writing check from the edit without turning one preference into a universal word ban.

- **Remove unnecessary first-person emphasis.** If the surrounding sentences already make the applicant the actor, `그래서 판단 기준을 제가 정하고` can read more naturally as `그래서 판단 기준을 정하고`. Keep `제가` or `저는` when the actor changes, responsibility is disputed, or omission makes the action ambiguous.
- **Let a specific unresolved question express the motivation.** Do not automatically end an answer with `[기업명]의 [프로젝트명]에서 ... 지원했습니다`. When the application context already identifies the position, a concrete, confirmed aim such as `어떤 조건에서 결과가 달라지는지 파악해 해결하고 싶습니다` may carry the motive better. Retain the employer name or explicit application reason if otherwise needed to answer the actual prompt; do not remove job fit merely to avoid formulaic prose.
- **State the bounded result with an active verb.** Compare `○○를 해결했다고 단정할 수는 없지만, 해당 점유를 줄인 것입니다` with `○○를 해결한 것은 아니지만, 불필요한 점유를 줄였습니다`. Prefer the latter when supported: it keeps the distinction about what did not happen while making the achieved change readable. Do not replace an honest caveat with an unsupported claim of performance improvement or measured savings.

After adopting such a user edit, do not restore the deleted first-person marker, employer-label ending, or nominalized hedge in a later polish unless the meaning or the question genuinely requires it.

Keep that coverage map through the final review: for every required subquestion and explicit user correction, identify the exact sentence or connected passage in the current complete answer that addresses it. A polished answer, relevant anecdote, or reviewer assertion does not fill a missing requested element. For a strengths-and-weaknesses question, verify both in the actual text; do not pass a strengths-only answer because the overall personality story sounds convincing. Distinguish optional examples in employer guidance from mandatory requirements, but treat a user's explicit request to include a particular element as an active constraint. Recheck coverage after shortening, reordering, or style edits. If a required element lacks evidence, flag the specific gap instead of inventing it. If it is missing from locked text, seek or use explicit revision authority for that field without changing unrelated approved answers.

When the question asks how necessary knowledge was acquired and applied, verify a connected passage naming what the applicant studied, the supported learning effort, and where that same knowledge informed an actual action. A course list beside a separate work example, chronology alone, or `배운 내용을 적용했습니다` does not establish that relationship. Keep learning and implementation attributed to the specific subject or technology: studying one to understand documentation does not establish implementing with it, and a later check does not prove use of everything listed in a course. Use a supported application example or identify the missing link separately; do not invent study, use, or results to complete the answer. Recheck the connection in the final text after shortening it, using the existing question-to-evidence map rather than a separate checklist of topic words.

For a question asking for personality words or values, select labels that the actual behavior supports. Accepting and applying a correction may show openness to feedback; it does not alone establish listening skill. Reducing repetitive work can show efficiency; initiative additionally needs evidence that the applicant identified or started the action. If employer values matter, verify the official wording and select among the applicant's confirmed traits rather than inventing a personality to match a slogan. The connection is an interpretation supported by the example, not proof that the employer will endorse it. Start with the behavior and determine what it demonstrates; do not choose an attractive label first and force an unrelated episode to support it. A label such as accuracy needs an actual distinction, criterion, or checking action that explains what was made more reliable, not simply effort, completion, or the presence of a test. If the requested label is not supported, say so in the review or choose another supported trait when authorized rather than manufacturing evidence.

For repository-backed examples, compare the initial description with the relevant final code, review changes, and actual test records when accessible. Do not repeat an initial proposal as the final behavior or infer that a test passed from its presence in source. If an essential source is inaccessible, state the evidence limit separately or narrow the claim. Explain the user-visible problem, the applicant's judgment, and what was actually checked in terms the intended hiring reader can follow. Transfer a checking method to an unfamiliar domain without claiming production experience, loss prevention, or safety guarantees that the source does not establish.

In an entry-level aspiration, distinguish a contribution within assigned responsibilities from authority to decide priorities, change a product, or commit a team. A personal interest in user feedback does not confer authority to implement every request. When supported by the role and the applicant's intention, describe carrying out assigned work accurately or promptly, communicating a concern, or proposing a change through the applicable process without inventing that process. Do not promise unilateral product changes, guaranteed speed, or broader business results. This does not prohibit initiative: preserve evidence of self-started improvement and legitimate proposals, and do not impose obedience as a universal cultural trait or assume an unpublished hierarchy.

## Verify duty evidence in the actual answer

For an answer about job competence or contribution, extend the question-to-evidence map into a required review record. For each relevant main duty in the current official posting, connect these four items in order:

1. The exact official duty wording and its source, date, and role scope.
2. The applicant's own confirmed evidence: what the person actually handled, how, and any supported result. Separate team technology and others' work from personal use or implementation.
3. The exact corresponding sentence or connected passage in the final answer being reviewed. Record an absent passage as missing; a keyword match or a reviewer explanation does not fill it.
4. The strength and limits of that connection: a supported contribution the applicant can make directly, a transferable action with relevant similarities and differences, a task that still requires learning, or an unconfirmed connection. Mark missing source or personal evidence explicitly instead of treating it as demonstrated ability.

Build the record from the available official duties and personal evidence before drafting, then fill and verify the passage entries against the complete revised answer. Coverage means assessing the relevant duties, not claiming mastery of all of them or forcing every duty into the submitted field. For an entry-level applicant, retain a concrete contribution within the demonstrated scope, explain a transferable method only as far as the evidence supports it, and keep unfamiliar work as a specific learning need when the question calls for it. Participation in a team that used a technology does not establish personal proficiency in that technology, and related practice does not establish professional experience in the employer's systems.

Make this a pass condition for review: where the question asks for competence and personal evidence exists, the actual answer must show the action, its basis, and its connection to the relevant duty. Merely saying that an experience is related to the role, listing duty or technology names, or promising to learn is insufficient. If the evidence record supports a contribution but the final passage contains only relevance or aspirations, rewrite that passage and repeat the comparison. Do not pass it because the intended relationship appears in private notes. If the evidence itself is missing, narrow the claim, use another supported experience when authorized, or identify the missing material separately; never invent work, technical mastery, results, or responsibility to complete the map.

Keep this mapping and its labels in the working review record unless the user asks to inspect them. Write the supported relationship naturally in the application answer; do not paste the internal table, official-duty inventory, or pass/fail explanation into the submitted prose. After any revision that changes a mapped passage, recheck its personal attribution, evidence strength, and duty connection before treating that version as reviewed.

## Ground application recommendations in role evidence

When writing a recommendation about whether to apply, reuse the official-duty and personal-evidence distinctions above. Eligibility for an entry-level posting does not by itself establish fit for its actual tasks. Explain which relevant work the applicant's own evidence supports directly, what transfers with limits, and what remains unconfirmed or is a confirmed mismatch. Missing evidence is not proof that the applicant lacks the ability. Judge against the published role and level; do not invent required tools or senior-level experience, or assume employer training will remove a gap. Existing application materials and a near deadline affect effort and timing, not suitability.

If the user requires independent suitability review before recommendations, apply the existing independent-review procedure to the recommendation and wait for a substantive result supporting suitability for the current role before recommending application. Requesting review, receiving a prose-only review, or holding a result for another role does not satisfy that condition. Reconcile material findings about role suitability with the current posting and personal evidence; do not turn unresolved suitability concerns or conditional findings into an unqualified endorsement. Minor wording preferences alone do not invalidate a supported suitability judgment. If the required result is pending or unavailable, say so and keep the recommendation pending. A suitability judgment or recommendation does not authorize submission.

## Review selection reasons, not only correctness

For a substantive application review, factual accuracy, grammar, and question coverage are necessary but not sufficient. Extend the official-duty evidence record to identify why the supported behavior gives this reader a reason to select the applicant and what concrete concern could still weaken that reason. Use published requirements and the supplied evidence, not invented competitors, unpublished hiring preferences, acceptance probabilities, or guarantees. Distinguish mandatory corrections (false claims, missing required answers, eligibility or format failures) from strategic improvements (weak differentiation, generic motivation, or an unconvincing work connection). Do not call a strategic weakness an automatic disqualification or inflate a wording preference into a requirement.

Before polishing individual sentences, test the whole answer against its exact prompt: identify the conclusion the answer owes the reader, the role of each paragraph, and the source-supported reasoning or choice that connects each selected case to that conclusion. Inspect the transition from the opening claim to the first example and from each example to the next claim. A locally clear success story does not pass if it answers a different question or leaves the reader to invent why it supports the stated choice. Repeated keywords such as improvement, users, or feedback do not establish that connection. Reorder or replace a poorly fitting case, or remove it when the remaining supported answer covers the prompt; do not rescue it by adding long background or an invented causal bridge. Then read every sentence as a hiring reader who has not seen the conversation, checking the necessary actor, context, reason, and transition. State the supported relationship in the answer itself, not solely in review notes. Distinguish why this job suits the applicant from why this company was chosen. A company feature or a recitation of assigned duties plus generic job interest does not establish a personal company choice; use confirmed personal motives and official company evidence, or identify the missing basis without inventing an interest. Apply the same evidence-fit test to other questions: collaboration needs confirmed interaction, feedback, coordination, or adaptation involving another person, not merely a solo task within a team; broad exposure to several topics does not prove learning speed without evidence of the learning-to-application interval or comparable context. Preserve genuine technical work and learning breadth without treating them as proof of a different claim.

Apply the [evidence, reason, and claim check](korean-language-principles.md#connect-evidence-reasons-and-claims) to the actual account: the experience and background, the applicant's supported interpretation, and the motive or contribution must form a readable relationship. Where future work is requested, keep the transferable action and its material limits concrete without inventing an intention or promising unassigned authority. These are semantic roles, not mandatory headings or a conclusion-last order.

Once a problem-solving example is justified by that whole-answer check, require only enough detail in the actual paragraph to make the causal chain recoverable: who is trying to do what, the specific condition that blocks that task, why that blockage matters to that task, what the applicant changed, and what was actually observed afterward. Introducing the service or adding a generic inconvenience label does not establish this chain. Check the opening claim against the example in the opposite direction as well: each promised benefit or problem type must be demonstrated by the evidence that follows. Narrow or remove unsupported claims about repeated effort, time savings, or user impact instead of inventing a story to support them. Keep the failure condition as narrow as the source: one unsupported input does not prove all alternative inputs fail or that a user must know an exact official term. Distinguish a reproduced test failure and result from observed user behavior or measured benefit. If a necessary link is unknown, identify it rather than making the experience more dramatic.

When the question asks how a past difficulty was overcome, connect the applicant's confirmed action to what actually changed in that difficulty. A lesson, a future intention, or the team's successful outcome alone does not establish the applicant's overcoming process. Preserve a supported partial improvement without declaring complete resolution. If only a lesson or proposed method is known, retrieve or ask for the missing application or outcome when needed; do not invent execution to complete the story. When the user's latest direct factual correction establishes current practice and an already realized change, use that account instead of an older summary describing only a future plan. Keep the correction within its confirmed scope without adding dates, duration, measured gains, or a finished deliverable. Read the final passage against the question: changing an ending to the past tense does not supply a missing action-result relationship. Apply the [clause-level tense guidance](korean-language-principles.md#past-events-current-views-and-general-statements), and preserve future goals when the question actually asks for them.

Challenge the strongest claim with a concrete adverse reading: what would make a reader doubt the applicant's own contribution, practical readiness, or fit? Identify the exact passage, relevant criterion, and evidence for that concern, then determine whether a supported revision can address it. Preserve demonstrated strengths rather than reducing the answer to apologies or learning wishes. For transferable experience, explain the usable action and its limits in the target work. For an actual gap or weakness, retain its present effect and a concrete confirmed mitigation or clearly labeled proposed plan; do not rewrite it as a disguised strength, declare it fully solved, or invent successful mitigation. A plan is not proof of execution.

When independent review is requested or established, cover both evidence and correctness and the selection/reader perspective, using separate reviewers where available and proportionate. Honor an explicitly required reviewer count or distinct review roles; one returned review does not satisfy a request for several independent reviews. Keep a version-specific completion record of assigned roles, returned findings, and resolved or pending material issues before claiming that requirement is complete. Include whole-answer structure and transitions in the review, not just the local problem chain. Give them the exact materials without the writer's verdict or desired conclusion. Do not require reviewers to find a fixed number of defects or agree with every criticism. The delivering assistant owns the final decision: reviewer PASS labels are inputs, not completion evidence. Independently read the exact assembled final answers against the questions, source facts, and latest constraints after integrating changes. For each concrete material objection, identify the affected passage and either repair it in the actual text and recheck it, reject the objection with source-backed reasoning, or leave the missing evidence explicitly unresolved; an acknowledgement or a better review explanation does not repair the answer. Assess factual accuracy separately from whether the text persuades by answering the question. Check whole-answer logic, sentence-level context, attribution, protected copy, and the complete current expression bans again, including wording introduced by reviewers. A review of earlier text or separate fragments does not pass the integrated result. Do not call an unresolved answer submission-ready. Stop when material issues are resolved or explicitly bounded and the supported selection reason is readable; no conceivable criticism is not a verifiable standard, and optional wording preferences do not justify endless rewriting. Honor the deadline reserve and version-freeze rules below.

For a preparation-and-strength answer, retain an explicit personal strength when removing a rejected sentence formula. A course, credential, ordinary class project, or routine statement about studying and checking results does not by itself distinguish that strength. Use the supported practice, additional study, change of approach, or evaluation that demonstrates it. An educational project remains a valid candidate when it contains those personal actions; do not exclude it solely because it came from a course. Do not infer persistence from one attempt, mastery from a score, or the causal effect of one change from a combined experiment. Recheck the complete answer after the style edit: the requested preparation and strength must both remain readable in the actual passage.

Adapt the problem-solving review to the actual question and experience. Reproduction, competing-cause checks, and verification can explain a technical investigation when supported, but they are not a mandatory debugging template for every kind of problem. Preserve the decisions, actions, and results needed to understand this case without inventing steps to complete a checklist. For a work-method improvement question, select the original burden, the applicant's supported choice or action, and what was checked or observed; a minor setup mistake and its repair need not dominate that explanation merely because they occurred. Retain the mistake when it explains a requested problem-solving decision, an important limitation, ownership, or the result. Omit incidental detail only when the remaining account stays accurate; do not claim an error-free process, hide an unresolved defect, or inflate the result. Judge relevance against the actual question rather than banning all failure accounts or automatically treating them as persuasive evidence.

If the question or user specifically calls for disagreement, show the confirmed shared goal, differing positions and reasons, the applicant's own coordinating action, and any supported compromise, renewed review, or agreement. A technical defect or ordinary suggestion alone does not establish that conflict. Preserve a later user clarification that the parties held different reasonable views; do not recast it as the applicant misunderstanding the other person's original instruction. Keep the requested final joint outcome, but do not turn a preceding improvement into the cause of release, approval, or success without evidence. Do not use this disagreement structure as the default for every cooperation question. When the current question asks for a shared goal, division of work, cooperation, and personal contribution, verify each in the actual answer, including how separate work was coordinated or combined when supported. An earlier approved conflict narrative for another question does not replace those requirements. Apply the latest authorized direction for the current answer while preserving unrelated locked fields; do not invent conflict, agreement, or team roles to fill the requested structure.

## Make the actor, object, and reason readable

Mark each achievement's owner before turning notes into first-person prose. Distinguish the applicant, collaborators, the team, and unrelated people whose work the applicant observed or studied. A repository owner, a shared project name, or a merged pull request alone does not prove who implemented a feature. Where another person's achievement remains relevant, attribute it explicitly at the point of use and state the applicant's own action separately. Do not let a later `저는` or an omitted subject claim the other person's result.

Name the supported action as well as its target when stating a role. A feature or tool followed by `맡았습니다` can leave development, design, operation, and testing indistinguishable. Restore the actual assigned or performed action instead of merely replacing `맡다` with `담당하다`; do not turn an assignment into completed implementation or claim work the source attributes to someone else. Keep a clear assignment when the action is already identifiable, and preserve an explicit correction requiring that action through later shortened drafts. For a proposal, make clear whom the applicant addressed and what they proposed when the reader cannot otherwise identify them. A vague noun such as `적용` or `도입` and a new synonym do not supply that missing content. Use only source-supported recipients and proposals, and distinguish proposing, acceptance, trial use, and adoption. Check the mechanism and consultation sequence before choosing a concrete verb: an automatic action triggered by an event is not necessarily a tool that colleagues operate together. When the source establishes asking permission to configure it, receiving agreement, and then configuring it, preserve that order and act instead of inventing a later invitation to use it. A corrected sequence replaces the earlier draft's interpretation; configuration alone still does not prove successful execution.

Apply the same attribution check to AI-assisted work. When the source supplies them, show the problem the applicant identified, the criteria or decisions they made, what they changed with AI assistance, and what they actually checked or observed. Merely listing requests or prompts, such as `AI에 수정을 요청했습니다`, may leave the reader unable to assess the contribution. Explain the supported work without hiding material AI assistance or presenting AI-generated code or text as unaided manual work. A request alone does not establish a successful modification, and an AI suggestion is not a verified result. Preserve those limits when that is all the source establishes; do not invent diagnosis, criteria, implementation, review, or effects to complete a success story. State each contribution once where it supports the answer rather than repeating an explanation of the same criterion.

Do not open by listing experiences as a self-definition in the form `~을 경험한 ~입니다`. Replacing that wrapper with `경험을 가진` or `역량을 갖춘` does not explain the applicant's contribution. Start with the source-supported action, relevant role, or reason the question needs, without inventing a profession, expertise, or effect. Keep a meaningful self-introduction when it identifies the actual speaker or role, and retain valid uses of the noun `경험` when the experience itself matters. Preserve exact approved copy unless its revision is explicitly authorized.

An explicit user ban on an expression applies throughout the requested manuscript, not only to its introduction or an awkward use. If the user prohibits `경험한`, check the complete revised manuscript for that exact substring, including occurrences inside longer words and later paragraphs; a contextually plausible use is not an exception. A prohibition on that expression does not by itself prohibit the noun `경험`. Apply a later explicit instruction authorizing changes to previously locked occurrences within its stated scope, while preserving other protected copy. Check the underlying meaning after rewriting as well as the banned substring; neither a synonym that retains an empty self-definition nor deletion of a necessary fact is a solution. Quotations used to explain a rule or construct a test fixture are distinct from the manuscript being delivered.

First check the actual reading context under the mandatory context companion, including explanations already visible in other application fields. Do not repeat an education or activity description the intended reader already has. At the first mention in a genuinely independently read answer, introduce an unfamiliar app's verified purpose and the applicant's role briefly. Preserve the actual action and object. In a fictional tool-care app, `공구별 손질 방법을 검색하는 앱` describes finding instructions; `공구를 찾는 앱` claims a different function. Do not infer a purpose from the name, and do not rely on a later answer or attachment to define it. Reuse the minimum introduction where needed without retelling the whole experience.

Connect technical detail to the supplied reason for a decision, the action taken, and its observed effect. Name the risk or constraint when it explains why the action mattered. A list of libraries, tests, or automation jobs cannot supply that relationship. Keep a technical name when it helps the reader assess the actual work; do not replace a supported action with vague praise or fill missing reasoning with an invented alternative. Preserve the distinction between a test's expected value, an executed test result, and an observed user outcome.

Repair nominalized motivation sentences at the meaning level. Phrases such as `~한 일을 다른 곳에서도 하고 싶습니다`, a noun pile ending in `개발을 맡습니다`, or a long duty modifier ending in `지원한 이유입니다` can hide the actual reason or break clause relationships. Name the supported action with a fitting verb and state why the applicant chose it; do not merely swap the ending while retaining an empty connection. These are review signals, not universal bans on `일`, `맞다`, or `맡다`. Keep a clear supported assignment such as `행사 진행을 맡았습니다` when appropriate, and respect an explicit user's narrower expression ban and protected copy.

When the user requests a more formal application voice, carry that preference through subsequent editable drafts in the same scope instead of reverting to casual word choices. For example, an explicit preference for `수정하다` over `고치다` should cover applicable wording about revising text or software, including inflected forms. Check the word's actual object and action: do not mechanically use `수정하다` for repairing a physical object, treating an illness, or another meaning it does not express. Choose a fitting formal verb when the broader register preference applies. Preserve exact quotations, protected names, and unrelated approved fields unless their revision is explicitly authorized. Formality does not require noun stacks, inflated claims, or stiff predicate-object combinations. Treat the requested preference as scoped user guidance, not a universal rule that ordinary Korean verbs are wrong.

Remove stock closing sentences that merely repackage an already described action as `~하는 것이 목표입니다` or `~한 것입니다`. End with the actual action or necessary content instead of replacing the wrapper with a similar slogan. When the question asks for goals or plans, retain the specific intended outcome and supported plan; removing a formula must not remove that answer or turn an intention into completed work. Judge the sentence's contribution, not just its ending: a genuine explanation using `~한 것입니다` may clarify a needed meaning or scope. Preserve locked wording and apply any explicit wording restriction within its stated scope.

Apply the [abstract usefulness check](korean-language-principles.md#abstract-usefulness-in-application-answers) to conclusions about a method or experience, including `~하는 데 활용할 수 있습니다`. Recheck the complete affected answer after rewriting or deleting the conclusion: the question's required action, result, and any requested future application must remain supported and readable. A synonym swap or an added first-person subject does not pass this check by itself.

Apply the context companion's [need check](context-audience-boundary.md#decide-what-belongs-in-the-result) before treating a true detail as useful evidence. Routine error handling or the continuation of an ordinary check is not a separate achievement merely because it occurred. Retain that detail when it explains the actual disputed choice, a material trade-off, a required constraint, or the result. If one sentence combines dispensable operational detail with a needed agreement or outcome, remove only the dispensable part. Distinguish rewording a claim from removing its meaning: once an unnecessary claim is removed, do not revive it with a synonym or an added contrast. An explicit deletion request applies within its scope, not as a universal ban on `보존하다`, `남기다`, or necessary explanations of retained information. Preserve the supported outcome, the user's confirmed reflection, and unrelated required facts without filling the space with invented feelings, actions, virtues, or results. Recheck the complete answer for question coverage and paragraph flow after the deletion.

If the user explicitly excludes intention endings such as `~려 합니다` and `~려고 합니다`, check the complete requested manuscript for those patterns as well as other current expression bans. Preserve the actual action, supported contribution, interest, and any genuine future plan through a context-appropriate rewrite. Do not merely substitute another stock intention formula, remove all future meaning, or turn an intended action into a completed result. A ban on a verb and its inflections requires checking the inflected occurrences too; a dictionary-form search alone is insufficient.

When the user requests a calmer application voice with fewer pledge-like statements, apply that preference across the application, not only to service-design answers. State completed personal actions as facts. For work the applicant has not yet been assigned, express a grounded contribution they could make or a supported interest without promising decisions, authority, or outcomes they do not control. Keep the actual action and its connection to the official duty visible: softening the tone must not retreat from supported capability into only wanting to learn. Do not mechanically end every sentence with `생각합니다`, turn completed work into uncertainty, or remove all intention. Retain genuine personal plans and commitments established by the source and needed by the question. Reapply the duty-evidence passage check above to the softened answer, rather than treating a gentler ending as sufficient.
